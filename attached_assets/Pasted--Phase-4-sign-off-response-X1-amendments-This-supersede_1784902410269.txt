# 1. Scientific positioning

X1 is now the flagship result.

The defensible conclusion is:

> For this fixed GPT-4.1 deployment and repeated-PD task, meaning-preserving prompt rewording moved observed behavior from one corner to the opposite corner. A single prompt therefore identifies the behavior of that prompt–model configuration, not a representation-invariant behavioral property of the model.

Do **not** state categorically that behavior is “not incentive-determined.” X1 establishes overwhelming prompt-surface sensitivity and overturns the Phase 3 incentive-insensitivity interpretation, but it does not yet establish that incentives cannot matter under other wording. D2 and E are designed to test that stronger proposition.

Preferred headline language:

> **Prompt wording dominated the tested incentive manipulation and rendered single-wording behavioral inference non-identifiable.**

A broader methodological statement is permitted:

> Any behavioral claim intended to generalize beyond its exact prompt requires representation-robustness evidence.

Do not claim that “most prior literature is invalid” or equivalent language unless that literature is separately and systematically audited. The paper may state that single-wording studies leave representation-general claims unidentified unless they provide an equivalent robustness design.

X1 must be labeled accurately as a **result-informed but prospectively registered extension**: it was conceived after Phase 3 results, registered before any X1 data, and then executed. Report the exact registration and first-evidence timestamps. In prose, explain what `postRegistered=false` means rather than relying on that field name.

# 3. Global Phase 4 analysis rules

These rules apply to D, E, X2, and F.

## 3.1 Independent unit

The independent unit is the complete episode/run.

For one-shot self-play, record both seat decisions, but analyze the episode-level cooperation mean:

\[
Y_i \in \{0,\;0.5,\;1\}.
\]

For repeated games, rounds are nested within trajectories. Transition-level analyses must use trajectory-clustered inference or a hierarchical model.

Never present the total number of calls, seat decisions, or round transitions as the number of independent subjects.

## 3.2 Registered verdict versus statistical interpretation

Preserve two explicit layers:

1. **registered mechanical verdict** under the frozen predicate;
2. **estimand-aware statistical interpretation**.

A zero sample standard deviation permits an exact statement about the recorded corpus. It does not remove uncertainty about the model-policy distribution.

## 3.3 Statistical methods

Do not default to Welch intervals for binary corner data.

For balanced factorial experiments, use episode-level randomized contrasts or a preregistered linear-probability factorial model with heteroskedasticity-robust inference. Report cell-level exact or cluster-aware intervals separately.

For conditional sequential behavior, resample or model complete trajectories rather than individual rounds.

Every predicate in the freeze packet must state:

- estimand;
- independent unit;
- contrast;
- direction or equivalence margin;
- interval/test construction;
- multiplicity treatment;
- conditional branches;
- model and prompt configuration to which it applies.

## 3.4 Multiplicity

Distinguish primary confirmatory predicates from secondary diagnostics.

Use Holm adjustment within any family containing multiple confirmatory contrasts, unless the predicate is a valid intersection–union conjunction. Do not turn every D1 coefficient into an independent confirmatory claim.

# 4. Experiment D — representation-robustness battery

## D1. Full PD presentation cross

Add wording \(W\) as a first-class factor.

Factors:

- **M — payoff matrix**
  - canonical: \((R,S,T,P)=(3,0,5,1)\);
  - affine: canonical ×3 +2;
  - novel-A: \((4,1,6,2)\);
  - novel-B: \((7,2,9,3)\).

- **W — wording family**
  - v1-style;
  - v2a-style.

  These are registered one-shot adaptations, not informal paraphrases generated during execution. Publish the exact pair and complete the semantic-invariance audit before sealing.

- **L — visible action labels**
  - semantic: “Cooperate” / “Defect”;
  - neutral: “J” / “F”.

- **O — option order**
  - cooperative-role first;
  - defect-role first.

- **P — payoff presentation order**
  - as defined;
  - permuted.

### Design decision

Use the **full 4 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 factorial**:

\[
64 \text{ cells} \times 10 \text{ episodes/cell} \times 2 \text{ calls}
= 1{,}280 \text{ calls/model}.
\]

Do not use the proposed 32-cell fraction.

The full cross costs exactly the same as 32 cells at 20 episodes/cell and avoids aliasing. That is now the correct trade because X1 makes representation interactions central to the paper. Primary factorial contrasts aggregate across many cells, so they retain substantially more than 10 episodes of effective support.

### Analysis

Use the episode-level cooperation mean as the outcome.

Freeze a factorial analysis containing:

- all main effects;
- all two-factor interactions.

The primary confirmatory family must contain only the theoretically loaded terms:

- \(W\) main effect;
- \(W \times M\);
- \(W \times L\);
- \(M \times L\).

Treat O, P, and their interactions as registered presentation diagnostics. Because the design is now complete, no O/P alias-resolution follow-up is needed.

The freeze packet must give the exact contrast coding for the four-level matrix factor. In addition to omnibus terms, specify planned interpretable contrasts, including at minimum:

- canonical versus affine;
- canonical versus the average of novel-A and novel-B;
- novel-A versus novel-B.

Do not rely only on omnibus p-values.

## D2. Words versus numerical incentives

Replace the partially redundant counterfactual-cell proposal with a complete three-factor design.

Factors:

- **W — wording**
  - v1-style;
  - v2a-style.

- **G — payoff structure**
  - canonical PD, in which the underlying defect-role is strictly dominant;
  - cooperation-dominant counterfactual:
    \[
    (R,S,T,P)=(5,4,3,1),
    \]
    in which the underlying cooperation-role is strictly dominant.

- **S — visible label-to-role mapping**
  - aligned: cooperation-role displayed as “Cooperate,” defect-role as “Defect”;
  - swapped: cooperation-role displayed as “Defect,” defect-role as “Cooperate.”

This gives:

\[
2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8 \text{ cells}.
\]

Run:

\[
8 \times 20 \text{ episodes} \times 2 \text{ calls}
= 320 \text{ calls/model}.
\]

Fix option and payoff presentation order in D2 to their registered canonical values so D2 isolates wording, numbers, and semantic labels. D1 already identifies order effects.

The engine must maintain immutable underlying action IDs. For every choice, store separately:

- underlying strategic role selected;
- visible label selected;
- payoff-dominant role;
- whether the visible word and numerical dominance align or conflict.

The parser must map the emitted response to the displayed action ID first. All analysis then derives strategic role from archived run metadata. This is essential in the swapped-label conditions.

### D2 estimands

Freeze planned contrasts for:

1. **Numerical responsiveness:** when the strictly dominant role changes, does choice move toward the newly dominant underlying role while wording and label mapping are held fixed?

2. **Surface-word responsiveness:** when “Cooperate” is moved from one underlying role to the other while payoffs and wording are held fixed, does choice follow the visible word?

3. **Relative surface versus numerical effect:** compare the magnitude of the label-mapping effect with the payoff-structure effect.

4. **Conflict-cell accuracy:** in cells where the word “Cooperate” points to the dominated role, report the probability of selecting:
   - the numerically dominant role;
   - the action visibly labeled “Cooperate.”

The prior \(\ge 0.80\) correct-dominant threshold may remain as a secondary strong-performance criterion, but support must require the lower confidence bound—not merely the observed proportion—to meet the threshold. If the planned sample size cannot support that claim except at a perfect corner, state that explicitly in the freeze packet.

The X1-informed directional prediction is that visible wording and labels may dominate numerical structure. The opposing outcome is equally publishable.

Approved with the following changes.

\[

\]

## 5.1 D-selected representation rule

Select the D1 cell whose episode-level round-one cooperation estimate is nearest 0.5 using **primary-model D1 data only**.

Use that exact representation for both the primary and cross-vendor model. Do not independently optimize a different representation for each model, because that would eliminate stimulus comparability.

Tie-break by the lowest preassigned sealed registry arm ID. Do not use an undefined “simpler representation” judgment after seeing D results.

The selected arm ID and selection calculation must be written to the event store before any E run begins.

## 5.2 Two-sided assay-sensitivity gate

The original gate was one-sided and would misclassify a ceiling as successful assay sensitivity.

For each presentation, the gate passes only if at least one δ cell is credibly away from both corners.

Use an uncertainty-aware rule:

> At least one δ cell must have its episode-level 95% interval wholly contained within \((0.05,0.95)\).

If neither cell passes:

- both near zero → **floor-confounded**;
- both near one → **ceiling-confounded**;
- opposite corners without an interior cell → report the observed discontinuity, but do not call the assay locally sensitive without a separately defined criterion.

The freeze packet must define the interval method for episode outcomes that can take 0, 0.5, or 1.

## 5.3 δ-slope predicate

For each presentation passing the gate, estimate:

\[
\Delta_\delta
=
P(C_1\mid\delta=.90)
-
P(C_1\mid\delta=.10).
\]

A positive slope is supported only if the 95% lower confidence bound for \(\Delta_\delta\) exceeds zero.

### Critical interpretation rule

Do not infer “δ-flat” or “incentive-insensitive” merely because the positive-slope predicate is not supported.

The verdict branches are:

1. **corner-confounded** — assay gate fails;
2. **positive δ slope detected** — gate passes and lower CI for \(\Delta_\delta\) exceeds zero;
3. **no positive slope detected / inconclusive** — gate passes but the CI includes zero.

A formal “δ-flat” conclusion requires a separately frozen equivalence margin and a sample size capable of placing the complete CI inside that margin. At 20 episodes per cell, this is unlikely for a substantively narrow margin. Do not add an equivalence claim without returning a power calculation and revised call budget for approval.

The primary E claim is the GPT-4.1 slope in the D-selected presentation. Community and cross-vendor slope tests are secondary and must use the stated multiplicity adjustment.

## 5.4 Historical v1 control

Phase 3’s v1 δ sweep may be cited as a historical registered result, but not treated as a contemporaneous randomized control for E.

Run the drift-sentinel battery immediately before and after E. If the v1/v2a corner fingerprint has changed, disclose that and do not pool Phase 3 with Phase 4 inference.

# 6. New Experiment X2 — wording-switch localization

X2 runs on the primary GPT-4.1 configuration only unless separately expanded.

The purpose is to localize the v1-to-v2a wording transition without overstating a path-dependent ladder result as a unique lexical cause.

## 6.1 Freeze the span decomposition first

Before any X2 data:

1. mechanically diff v1 against v2a;
2. divide the differences into \(k\) atomic semantic spans;
3. assign each span a fixed index;
4. publish the exact v1 and v2 text for every span;
5. seal the replacement operation and all resulting prompts in registry v3.

Span boundaries may not be revised after X2 execution begins.

The freeze packet must include:

- \(k\);
- exact span list;
- exact rung prompts;
- evidence that every intermediate prompt remains grammatical;
- a semantic-invariance checklist for every rung.

## 6.2 Complementary screening ladders

Use two complementary paths with the same registered span order.

Forward path:

\[
F_i =
\text{v1 with spans }1\ldots i\text{ replaced by v2a},
\quad i=0,\ldots,k.
\]

Complementary reverse path:

\[
R_i =
\text{v2a with spans }1\ldots i\text{ reverted to v1},
\quad i=0,\ldots,k.
\]

This construction is intentional. Do not use a reverse order that merely recreates the forward ladder in reverse.

The two endpoints are the existing X1 corners. The new prompt count is therefore:

\[
2(k-1)
\]

intermediate rungs.

Run 10 episodes per new rung at \(\delta=0.90\), using the X1 environment-seed list and matched horizon draws. These are matched environment conditions, not seeded model generations unless provider seeding exists.

The ladder stage is **screening/exploratory**. Plot the complete response curves with episode-level intervals.

A candidate switch is any adjacent rung pair whose observed absolute round-one cooperation difference is at least 0.50.

If several qualify, select exactly one confirmation candidate using:

1. largest absolute adjacent difference;
2. lowest span index as the tie-break;
3. forward path before complementary path as the final tie-break.

## 6.3 Held-out confirmatory minimal pair

The selected pair must be rerun on fresh, previously unused environment seeds:

- 20 new episodes on each side of the exact minimal pair;
- same payoffs, δ, horizon mechanism, provider configuration, and parser;
- prompts differ only in the selected span.

The confirmatory predicate is:

\[
|\Delta_{\text{span}}| > 0.50
\]

with the one-sided 95% lower confidence bound on the absolute difference also above 0.50, and with the direction matching the screening estimate.

This held-out stage—not the selected screening contrast—is the confirmatory evidence.

If no screening contrast reaches 0.50, X2’s registered existence claim is refuted and no candidate confirmation runs.

If the two paths identify different spans, or the same span acts differently by context, report path dependence. Do not call one phrase “the unique minimal lexical switch.” Use “switch-bearing span under the tested context” unless necessity and sufficiency are both established.

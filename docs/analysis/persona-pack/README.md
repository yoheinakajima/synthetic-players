## Interior structure

14/96 cells interior; 7 coop-leaning / 7 defect-leaning — interiority is
NOT a leaning phenomenon. Interior cells concentrate in 7 of 16 personas:
p05 (3), p13 (3), p04 (2), p07 (1), p12 (1), p15 (3), p02 (0)... nine
personas are pure-corner everywhere. Factor pattern of the interior-heavy
personas: p05 (competitive-patient-averse, 35), p13
(competitive-patient-averse, 61), p15 (competitive-impulsive-averse, 63) —
**competitive + risk-averse dominates the interior list**, while the
"agreeable-patient" personas sit at the cooperate ceiling and the
"competitive-risk-seeking" personas at the defect floor. Tension between
factors, not any single factor, appears to produce interior behavior.

## p13 (Harper) — the δ-slope persona

Competitive, patient, risk-averse, 61, landscape gardener. s2a cells: d90
0.750 [0.428, 0.945], d10 0.333 [0.099, 0.651] — both interior, slope LB
+0.083 > 0 (Family-E assay, the only pass in the program). Note the factor
reading: *patient* + a δ-slope is exactly the economics — the patient
persona responds to the shadow of the future. Registered leaning:
cooperative? No — p13 is coop-leaning by the ≥2 rule (patient +
risk-averse) yet competitive; its cells sit mid-interior rather than at
the coop ceiling.

## Swap-cell dissociation (word vs payoff)

All 16 personas choose the COOPERATE-worded (payoff-dominant) option at
0.68–1.00 (bare: 0.00). Both leanings, no gap. Two readings, not separable
in this design: (i) personas confer incentive sensitivity; (ii) personas
lexically attract to the prosocial word. The coop-leaning descriptive probe
cannot discriminate because both mechanisms point the same way in this
cell. Registered follow-up would need an anti-aligned cell (prosocial word
on the dominated option) — out of scope under the seal.

# Round 2 — editorial and framing reviews

> **SOURCE STATUS:** Living summary of two additional Round 2 reviews that accompanied the methods review. The original texts were supplied in conversation. This summary records the adopted editorial dispositions without presenting them as independent experimental evidence.

## Title and abstract discipline

The editorial reviews agreed that the paper should not spend its title or abstract on the fragile single-persona result. They recommended:

- retiring “Level-Matching Is Cheap” and “Moment Matching Is Cheap,” because exact condition-specific mean matching would mechanically recover the aggregate contrast;
- keeping “coarse” explicit in the title;
- removing p13 from the abstract and contribution list;
- avoiding “deterministic,” “not payoff-determined,” and “fixed, drift-free population” language;
- stating that semantic cues can override payoff dominance in conflict cells while payoff sensitivity remains representation-conditional.

## Structural decisions

The reviews favored a three-layer architecture:

1. empirical fixed-panel result;
2. composition/identification and representation mechanism;
3. auditability and correction record.

They supported retaining only an excerpt of the sealed discussion plus a current-status correction table in the main text, with the complete sealed artifact in the supplement. They also supported keeping the Dal Bó–Fréchette literature as explicitly protocol-nonmatched context rather than as a quantitative effect benchmark.

## Scope and falsification

The reviews endorsed a deliberately narrow claim about lightweight persona sentences. Rich interview-conditioned agents were identified as the strongest likely falsifier of any broader “conditioning is cheap” interpretation. The resulting manuscript treats Park-style self-report grounding as a distinct, potentially more successful construction rather than evidence against the specific lightweight-panel failure mode.

## Attribution

The reviewers supported a responsibility-based convention: the human author accepts responsibility for every claim; the autonomous pipeline is described as apparatus; and adopted intellectual contributions from adversarial AI reviewers are disclosed by function. Later rounds expanded this record to include reviewer-role transitions and independent verification.

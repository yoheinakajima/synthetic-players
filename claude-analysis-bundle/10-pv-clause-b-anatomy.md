## The confound, stated plainly

In the swap cell the option displayed under the word COOPERATE is the
payoff-dominant option. "Refuses the dominated option" and "follows the
COOPERATE word" are therefore **the same physical choice**. The sealed
predicate keys on the choice; it cannot distinguish incentive-driven
refusal from lexical word-following. Every clause-(b) pass below carries
this ambiguity.

## What the recorded data can and cannot decide

- **Can:** the *choice-level* claim — personas overwhelmingly take the
  payoff-dominant / COOPERATE-worded option (bare swap twin
  `p4-d2-w1-can-sw-gpt`: 0/40 refusals — the bare subject follows the word
  to the dominated option; every persona reverses that). The direction of
  the persona effect at the choice level is unambiguous.
- **Cannot:** the *mechanism* — whether personas respond to the payoff
  structure or the label. The de-confounding cell (balanced payoffs ×
  persona, where word and dominance dissociate) **was never run** and is
  scope-sealed to future work (`docs/paper/scope-seal.md`).
- **Cross-reference (bare anchor):** Phase 4's bare word-vs-payoff probe
  (P4-D2-3, aligned/balanced coding) found 0 word-driven refusals in both
  models (CP [0.000, 0.088] each; `docs/phase4/d2-report.md`), and Phase 4
  D2 established bare word-following in the swap cell for gpt (word-choice
  mean 0.0 on the role scale = 100% word-side). Personas invert the bare
  subject's swap behavior; which lever they pull remains open.
- The coop-leaning × swap descriptive probe (outcome-blind decision D2)
  shows the same word-side choice for cooperative-leaning personas —
  *consistent with* lexical operation, but not dispositive (word and
